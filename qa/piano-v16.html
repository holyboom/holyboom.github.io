The thinking behind the sound

The field uses restrained tonal foundations and seeded, filtered electrical texture. Slow asynchronous modulation gives it movement without a lead melody. Scroll controls one continuous filament: smoothed speed shapes density, direction changes openness, and assembly progress opens its spectrum. The same current stays with you after Assemble. Skills are a little more open, the gallery is restrained, About makes room for reading, and Contact settles into a softer return. Arrival cues are tied to actual visual docking, not an arbitrary section boundary. Identical selections stay silent, and fast crossings do not queue a burst of sounds. The accepted Assemble settle/reconnect cues remain.

The piano phrases use recorded Salamander Grand Piano keys by Alexander Holm (CC BY 3.0), trimmed, filtered, repitched, arranged and mixed with original section textures. The piano is not a synthesizer beep. Small open intervals share an A-based palette with the existing field. The sound buses share one AudioContext. Ambient and scroll yield when a brain reaction occurs; silence and manual controls stay intentional. Background ambience and scroll response can be disabled independently in the showcase’s Sound mix. Muting sound never stops Motion.

Sound requires an explicit tap. A hidden or interrupted page stops its audio; return requires another tap. This soundscape does not promise an emotional, therapeutic or engagement outcome. First meaningful control actions can also start sound; scrolling alone cannot. Physical iPhone speaker quality and subjective pleasantness still require listening on the device.

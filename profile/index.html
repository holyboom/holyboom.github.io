Before AI · the urge to design

As a child, I wanted to be an architect—to design spaces people could step into and inhabit.

  1. 01Imagine a place
  2. 02Draw the first lines

That early pull toward design stayed with me when I chose to study software engineering. Frontend became my creative space—a place where code took visible form, where people could touch, test, and make it their own.

I care about the character of what I build. The proportion of an interface, the rhythm of its interactions, and the clarity of its next step all shape how it feels to use. Software simply gave that same architectural impulse a different medium.

04 / THE POSSIBILITY BECOMES PERSONAL

2024Beginning AI development while still working at ttb

I could see it.
I wanted to build
something to try.

I began developing my AI capabilities while I was still at ttb. What I believed still needed to take shape as something people could test in real work.

  1. 01See something worth building
  2. 02Put the belief to the test
  3. 03Build something people can try

Some people around me were not yet convinced, including people working in the same field. I wanted to give them something they could actually try. My belief alone could only take the idea so far.

I started with the work in front of me: bringing GPT into Jira, Confluence and Bitbucket workflows, with people reviewing the outputs. The possibility I had imagined began to present real problems I had to work through.

A model’s answer was a starting point. I still needed to understand where it came from, what was missing, and where a person should stop and inspect it. Working through those details showed me the distance between an answer and a tool someone could trust enough to use.

05 / ANOTHER SIDE OF THE BEGINNING

A closer look at the AI work that began at ttb

Not everyone
saw it yet.
I kept building.

As I began learning and developing AI, some people around me did not yet see the same possibility.

  1. 01Hold on to the possibility
  2. 02Meet the resistance
  3. 03Stay with the problem
  4. 04Return to the work
  5. 05Keep the light alive

Even peers in the same discipline were unconvinced—and fairly so, while the idea existed mostly in theory. To make it tangible, I started building on my own, working through each obstacle one piece at a time.

Every skill and workflow I create traces back to those hands-on encounters. I test an approach against a real problem, examine where it breaks, look for sharper methods, and return to refine the next version. Week after week, that steady cadence became HolyMind.

Every difficult part asked something of me. Every return to the work left something I could build on.

Once a lesson holds up in practice, I look outward. I study research, engineering patterns, and tools from around the world, selecting and combining the strongest methods I can find for the problem in front of me.

Through repeated testing and trimming, each skill earns its own purpose, method, and character. Connected into workflows, those capabilities give new work a stronger foundation from the start.

That is the moat I am building: not a secret shortcut, but lived experience, deliberate curation, and revision after revision until a capability proves its worth in real use.

07 / WHERE IT COMES TOGETHER

Today · design, software and AI together

The soul of
AI experiences.

I want people to feel the possibility, without carrying all the complexity.

  1. 01Face the hidden complexity
  2. 02Shape a clear way in
  3. 03Give the person agency
  4. 04Make the experience inviting

Working with AI brings my frontend instincts and software engineering background together in a wider creative space. I can shape both the underlying machinery of a tool and the way someone enters, understands, and works with it.

The interface should absorb the heavy complexity behind the scenes while keeping the next step clear, the interaction inviting, and the person in control. Reasoning, sources, and moments for human review remain within reach whenever they are needed.

Thoughtful design opens the door, but care in every detail of use is what makes people want to return. To me, that is the heart of AI experiences.
